Responsible AI, Ethics, & Clinical Boundary Governance

1. Scope & Non-Clinical Purpose

This system is strictly designed as an administrative decision-support tool. It predicts operational scheduling vulnerability to assist clinic staff in directing reminder outreach (SMS, automated IVR, or phone calls) and managing schedule buffers. Under no circumstances is this model permitted to predict diagnosis, clinical urgency, or medical outcomes, nor may it be used to deny care or charge punitive cancellation fees.

2. Point-in-Time Integrity & Leakage Prevention

Predictions use solely data available at booking confirmation time (lead days, booking weekday, prior visit history, distance, and patient age). No post-appointment outcome data or downstream diagnostic notes are included in the feature pipeline.

3. Mitigating Algorithmic Demographic Bias

Socioeconomic factors (e.g. welfare/scholarship status) and commute distance reflect real-world structural transportation barriers. Flagging high-risk patients should prompt proactive clinic support—such as offering telehealth alternatives, transit assistance, or flexible afternoon slots—rather than punitive double-booking or scheduling deprioritization.

4. Failure Modes & Human-in-the-Loop Safeguards

False Positives (patient flagged as potential no-show, but shows up) result only in courteous reminder calls, which patients typically appreciate. Every high-risk action requires human review before buffer slots are confirmed.
